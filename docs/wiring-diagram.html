Seismograph Wiring

Five jumpers, one breadboard. An MPU6050 on I²C, a pot for full-scale deflection, a button to print, and an LED that lights when the trace clips — then the ESP32 talks to the thermal printer over Bluetooth, with no wire between them at all.

Where the wiring sits

Only one leg of this chain is wired. Everything downstream of the ESP32 is radio, which is why the printer never shares a ground with the board.

The breadboard

Every connection

Power comes off the board once, into a 3V3 rail and a GND rail; every part taps those rather than fighting for pins. Filled dots are joints. The little hump is one wire hopping another without touching it.

Details

The three parts that get wired wrong

Each of these boots fine, compiles fine, and misbehaves in a way that looks like a firmware bug.

The pot needs all three legs

A potentiometer only reads as a position when it is a divider — 3V3 at one end, GND at the other, wiper in the middle. Wire just the wiper and one end and GPIO34 is a floating input behind a resistor: it has no internal pull-down, so it drifts with whatever your hand is near.

LED polarity is silent

The short leg on the flat side of the rim is the cathode, and it goes to ground. Backwards, the LED never lights and nothing else changes — the firmware still thinks it is driving a trigger indicator, so you end up debugging the threshold instead of the leg.

Wire the button across, not along

Two of a tact switch's four pins are permanently joined inside the body. Land both jumpers on one joined pair and GPIO27 sits at ground forever — the firmware reads a button held down since boot and prints on its own. Straddle the breadboard's centre channel and the geometry sorts itself out.

A1 = ADC1, analog that keeps working with Wi-Fi up. A2 = ADC2. in = input-only, no internal pull-up — right for a pot, useless for the button.

The pot must stay on GPIO32–39

ADC2 — GPIO0, 2, 4, 12–15, 25–27 — stops working the moment the Wi-Fi driver starts, and it fails silently, returning garbage rather than an error. Moving the pot to GPIO25 to tidy up the breadboard would look completely fine and read nonsense. GPIO34 is also input-only with no internal pull-up, which is exactly right for a pot and exactly wrong for the button.

Placement

Mounting and power

Couple it to the thing you are measuring

Tape or hot-glue the MPU6050 flat against the desk or floor. A module hanging on its jumpers measures the resonance of the jumpers — a much larger and much less interesting signal than the one you want.

Orientation matters: the module's Z axis is the seismogram trace, so mount it flat with Z pointing up. X and Y only feed the horizontal-peak stat printed in the header.

Power

USB into the ESP32 is fine — the whole circuit draws a few tens of milliamps. The printer runs off its own internal battery and never draws from the board; the only thing between them is Bluetooth.
